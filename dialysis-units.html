Dialysis Units

In-center and home dialysis support, managed by experienced clinical teams.

What is dialysis?

Dialysis is a treatment that takes over your kidney functions when those organs stop doing their job. There are different types, or "modalities," of dialysis. The most common is In-Center Hemodialysis, where patients generally go three times per week. Other options include peritoneal dialysis, home hemodialysis, and nocturnal in-center hemodialysis.

The type of dialysis a patient receives helps determine where they go for regular treatments and how often. Treatments are typically done at a specialized hemodialysis center or at home. Our physicians and nurse practitioners see our dialysis patients regularly at all of the dialysis unit locations.

Dialysis Modalities

Care designed around your life

In-Center Hemodialysis

Treatment at a specialized center, usually three times per week, with each session lasting 3–5 hours.

Peritoneal Dialysis

Uses a special fluid placed in the belly to gently filter waste and excess fluid from the blood.

Home & Nocturnal Options

Home hemodialysis and nocturnal in-center options give you more flexibility and control over your schedule.
